## PART A — STRONG / PEER-REVIEWED OR ACADEMIC EVIDENCE

### A1. Amazon distribution centers specifically: ~10% price drop within 0.5 mile
- **Claim:** New Amazon distribution facilities depress nearby single-family house prices by almost 10% within 0.5 mile (6% with a simpler model).
- **Source:** "Prime Locations, Hidden Costs: Measuring the Impact of Amazon Distribution Centers on Housing Prices," Jeffrey G. Robert, Fernando Rios-Avila, Velma Zahirovic-Herbert, Karen M. Gibler. SSRN working paper, posted Oct 2024. Staggered difference-in-differences across facility openings; authors state the effect "consistently depresses local single-family property values."
- **URL:** https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4990815 (DOI 10.2139/ssrn.4990815)
- **Status:** **UNVERIFIED (fetch blocked — SSRN CAPTCHA).** Numbers are from the SSRN abstract as surfaced by search. Working paper, not yet peer-reviewed as far as found. Gibler and Zahirovic-Herbert are established real-estate academics.
- **Use on site:** Lead headline stat, but caption it honestly as a 2024 university working paper; download the PDF and quote the abstract verbatim.

### A2. Industrial sites: 14.9% lower within 250 m (820 ft); effect measurable out to ~1,093 m (0.68 mi)
- **Claim:** Houses within 250 metres of an industrial site sell for 14.9% less than houses beyond 2,250 m, all else equal; the drag decays and "fades out" beyond about 1,093 m; larger sites affect a wider ring ("the larger the site, the larger the range of houses which are affected").
- **Source:** Friso de Vor & Henri L.F. de Groot, "The Impact of Industrial Sites on Residential Property Values: A Hedonic Pricing Analysis from the Netherlands," *Regional Studies* 45(5): 609–623 (2011); working-paper version Tinbergen Institute TI 2009-035/3. Sample: 2005 house sales in Randstad + Noord-Brabant, NVM transaction data.
- **URL (verified, full text extracted):** https://papers.tinbergen.nl/09035.pdf ; journal: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00343401003601925
- **Use on site:** "Peer-reviewed: homes within ~800 ft of an industrial site sell for ~15% less." The 4.26M sq ft size makes the "larger site → wider ring" finding directly relevant.

### A3. Truck traffic specifically: a 1% rise in truck traffic cuts a $420k home's value by $2,000–$2,750 (100–400 m band)
- **Claim:** For 4,715 single-family homes near the LA/Long Beach port freeway corridor, a 1% increase in truck traffic reduces a $420,000 house located 100–400 m from the freeway by $2,000–$2,750; a 1% increase in *total* traffic reduces value by only $24. Truck traffic, not cars, is what buyers punish.
- **Source:** Wei Li & Jean-Daniel Saphores (UC Irvine), "Assessing Impacts of Freeway Truck Traffic on Residential Property Values: Southern California Case Study," *Transportation Research Record* 2288(1): 48–56 (2012), DOI 10.3141/2288-06.
- **URL (verified via DOI abstract mirror and UCI ITS listing):** https://doi.org/10.3141/2288-06 ; https://its.uci.edu/?p=70788
- **Use on site:** Pair with the filing's 555–809 truck trips/day, 24/7: each truck-traffic percentage point is a measurable, peer-reviewed dollar loss.

### A4. Highway/traffic noise: 8–10% price reduction; ~0.40% per decibel
- **Claim:** Survey of nine US/Canadian hedonic studies: "traffic noise on a main highway decreases the price of a house by between 8 and 10 per cent." Separate 1982 meta-analysis of 17 estimates: noise depreciation index 0.16–0.63% per dB, mean 0.40% per dB.
- **Sources:** J.P. Nelson, "Highway Noise and Property Values: A Survey of Recent Evidence," *Journal of Transport Economics and Policy* 16(2), 1982 — https://jtep.org/journal/highway-noise-and-property-values-a-survey-of-recent-evidence/ (verified). National Academy of Engineering, *Technology for a Quieter America* (2010), ch. 7 — https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/12928/chapter/9 (verified).
- **Use on site:** "Every extra decibel of truck noise ≈ 0.4% off every home in earshot." Cross-link to the noise page.

### A5. Freeway adjacency: 6.8% (0–200 ft) and 4.6% (200 ft–½ mile) lower price per sq ft over 16 years
- **Claim:** Along US-60 (Superstition Freeway, Phoenix), homes 0–200 ft from the freeway averaged 6.8% lower price/sq ft than the control area over 16 years; homes 200 ft to ½ mile averaged 4.6% lower.
- **Source:** Jason Carey, *Impact of Highways on Property Values: Case Study of the Superstition Freeway Corridor*, Arizona DOT Final Report 516 (Oct 2001), with FHWA. Also published as Carey & Semmens, TRR 1839 (2003).
- **URL (verified, PDF extracted):** https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/37313/dot_37313_DS1.pdf
- **Use on site:** Distance-band evidence that the penalty extends to a half-mile, not just abutting lots.

### A6. Homes within 500 ft of an interstate sell for less (spatial-econometric confirmation)
- **Claim:** 8,010 qualified sales 2012–2016 within one mile of I-295, Jacksonville FL: the 0–500 ft band variable was "negative and significant at 99 percent" in all models; effect not significant at 500–1,500 ft. Lit review: "highway proximity is a negative externality on home prices, and this phenomenon holds across time and locale." Also cites Kilpatrick et al.: homes within 300 ft of a transit corridor are negatively affected, and Hughes & Sirmans: price reduction correlates with traffic count.
- **Source:** McElveen (MAI, CCIM, CRE), Brown & Gibbons, "Highway Noise and Elevation Effects on Nearby Home Prices: Spatial Econometrics Using LIDAR-Derived Data," *Real Estate Finance* 37(1), Summer 2020.
- **URL (verified, PDF extracted):** https://urbaneconomics.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/REF_Summer20_McElveen-Brown-Gibbons.pdf
- **Use on site:** Modern (2020) confirmation by a practicing MAI appraiser that the closest ring bears the loss.

### A7. Urban Institute: roadways reduce nearby property values; health effects concentrated within 150–300 m (500–1,000 ft)
- **Claim:** "Roadways also tend to reduce the property values of nearby residences." Health effects (lung disease, stroke, premature birth) are "most common within 300 meters (about 1,000 feet)"; within 100 m, 5% higher acute MI, PM2.5/NOx 30–37% higher at schools; within 200 m, 8% higher stroke mortality odds at 150 m.
- **Source:** Urban Institute, *The Polluted Life Near the Highway* (Nov 2022).
- **URL (verified, PDF extracted):** https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/2022-11/The%20Polluted%20Life%20Near%20the%20Highway.pdf
- **Use on site:** Bridges the property-value page to the air/health page; the 1,000-ft radius maps onto the abutting homes and Brightview at 1,200 ft.

### A8. Federal Highway Administration (official position)
- **Claim:** "There are strong indications that [highway noise] does [reduce property values] when the property use is incompatible with the highway, as in the case of many residential areas."
- **URL (verified):** https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/Environment/noise/noise_compatible_planning/federal_approach/audible_landscape/al07.cfm
- **Use on site:** A federal agency admitting the effect.

### A9. Warehouse-generated truck trips and neighborhood externalities (context)
- **Claim:** A 500,000 sq ft facility generates up to 300 truck trips/day; 5+ axle trucks are over two-thirds of warehouse trips; heavy-duty vehicles produce 90% of on-road diesel PM in California.
- **Source:** Quan Yuan (Tongji Univ.; formerly USC), "Bearing the Brunt of Expanding E-Commerce," *Transfers Magazine* Issue 6, Fall 2020.
- **URL (verified):** https://transfersmagazine.org/magazine-article/issue-6/bearing-the-brunt-of-expanding-ecommerce/
- **Use on site:** Sanity-check the operator-supplied 555 trucks/day for a 4.26M sq ft building (Yuan's ratio implies far more).

### A10. South Bronx delivery warehouse: measured overnight truck increase
- **Claim:** After a delivery warehouse opened (summer 2018), overnight truck traffic 9 PM–midnight rose 31.7% (95% CI 23.4–40.6%) at one site and 27.7% at another; modest measured noise/black-carbon increases.
- **Source:** Shearston et al., *Int. J. Environ. Res. Public Health* (2020).
- **URL (verified):** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7246477/
- **Use on site:** NY-specific, peer-reviewed proof that the truck traffic is overnight — the exact hours that drive noise-based value loss.

### A11. EDF "New York Warehouse Boom" (context)
- **Claim:** 4.8 million New Yorkers (≥1 in 4) live within ½ mile of a warehouse ≥50,000 sq ft; 649,000 of them over 64; warehouses "attract hundreds of thousands of freight truck trips daily."
- **URL (verified):** https://www.edf.org/media/report-1-4-new-york-residents-lives-within-half-mile-mega-warehouse (Jan 18, 2024).
- **Use on site:** Frames the ½-mile radius as the standard impact zone used by researchers.

## PART B — APPRAISAL / REAL-ESTATE INDUSTRY SOURCES

### B1. Appraisal Institute definition of external obsolescence (verified)
- **Text:** *The Dictionary of Real Estate Appraisal*, 6th ed. (Appraisal Institute, 2015): external obsolescence is "a type of depreciation; a diminution in value caused by negative external influences and generally incurable on the part of the owner, landlord, or tenant." Three recognized categories (Williams, *Appraisal Journal* 1996): locational, **environmental ("properties affected by a noxious nearby use")**, and economic. Measurement methods per *The Appraisal of Real Estate*, 15th ed.: paired-sales analysis or capitalized income loss.
- **Source:** Stanley D. Longhofer, PhD, "Land Values and External Obsolescence," *The Appraisal Journal*, Spring 2021, pp. 95–.
- **URL (verified, PDF extracted):** https://www.appraisalinstitute.org/getattachment/4dc20c66-65db-44cd-9acb-2c36e8c6e8f2/2021-spring-feat2-external-obsolescence.pdf
- **Use on site:** "The appraisal profession has a name for what Amazon would do to your house: external obsolescence — and by definition you can't fix it."

### B2. Industry rules of thumb (secondary, cite cautiously)
- Bankrate/Fox Business "External obsolescence devalues property": nearby industrial uses/noise corridors are textbook examples; "ugly, smelly, loud neighbors dent home values up to 10%." https://bankrate.com/real-estate/external-obsolescence-devalues-property (**UNVERIFIED — snippet only**).
- Opendoor 2025 "How Busy Roads Impact Property Values": cites Urban Institute 4–10% highway reduction and 0.3–0.8% per dB. https://www.opendoor.com/articles/understanding-how-busy-roads-affect-home-values (**UNVERIFIED — snippet only**).

## PART D — COUNTER-EVIDENCE THE APPLICANT WILL CITE

### D1. Cunningham (*Journal of Policy Analysis and Management*, 2025): Amazon entry raises metro home values 5.6%
- Amazon entry into a **metro area** raises employment rate 1.0 pt, wages 0.7%, home values 5.6%, rents 1.1%, utilities 6.0%. **Unit of analysis is the whole metro** — says nothing about homes adjacent to a facility, and the 5.6% is a *cost* to buyers/renters. Long Island already has ~9 Amazon sites, so the marginal "metro entry" effect is essentially zero here.
- **URLs:** https://reason.com/2024/12/16/amazon-warehouses-benefit-local-economies-study-finds/ (verified); JPAM: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/pam.70065 (**fetch blocked**); PDF: https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6508f15493db9d26ceaa10e8/t/6723d04dbe23467fa92d1c27/1730400333616/cunningham_jmp_24.pdf
- **Rebuttal line:** A1 measures the *neighborhood*; Cunningham measures the *region*. Both can be true: the region gains, the abutters pay.

### D2. Rutgers CAIT / NAIOP-NJ (Nov 2025): warehouses support 1.35M NJ jobs, $113B income
- **Commissioned by NAIOP New Jersey** (industrial developers' trade group). Contains **no property-value analysis**. https://re-nj.com/outsized-impact-new-jersey-warehouses-support-1-35-million-jobs-113-billion-in-personal-income-study-finds/ (verified).

### D3. GMU Center for Regional Analysis (Nov 2025): homes nearer data centers sold higher (N. Virginia, 2023)
- Not warehouses; authors attribute premium to "strong infrastructure… proximity to jobs," and the study "doesn't isolate data center proximity as a causal factor." Data centers generate no truck traffic. https://schar.gmu.edu/news/2025-11/study-home-prices-are-higher-when-house-near-data-center (verified).

### D4. Kawamura & Mahajan (TRR 1924, 2005, Chicago arterials): truck traffic variables statistically insignificant
- Used *assessed values* (not sales) along Chicago arterials; total traffic modest negative, trucks insignificant. Older, weaker data than Li & Saphores 2012. https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0361198105192400109 (**fetch blocked**).

### D5. "Costco effect" (UBC, 84 openings): +7.9% within 5 miles in higher-income counties — retail, not logistics; expect it anyway.

**Homes near the site (estimate — no parcel count found; label as such):** Holbrook averages ~1,410 units/sq mi. A ½-mile radius is 0.785 sq mi and a 1-mile radius 3.14 sq mi, but the site sits on Sunrise Hwy at the CDP's southern edge and the 140-acre site plus Costco/commercial strip occupy part of each circle. Assuming roughly half of each circle is Holbrook residential land: **~550 homes within ½ mile; ~2,200 within 1 mile.** Get a real count from Suffolk County GIS parcel data before publishing.

**Tax mechanics to state on the site:** NY assessments must be a uniform percentage of market value (RPTL §305); Islip assesses at a small fraction (RAR ~6.7%) of full value and a homeowner grieves by showing market-value evidence (RP-524 to the Board of Assessment Review, then SCAR for a $30 fee, or Article 7 certiorari). If hundreds of neighbors win reductions, the school/town levy does not shrink — it shifts onto everyone else in the district, so even residents far from the site pay for the value loss. (SCAR overview: https://ww2.nycourts.gov/litigants/scar/index.shtml ; ORPTS guide https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/publications/orpts/cert.pdf.)
